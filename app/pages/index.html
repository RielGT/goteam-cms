/ how I work

Simple. No surprises.

I keep the process boring on purpose. The fun part is your product.

01

I start with a real conversation.

Free 30-minute call. You tell me what you're building and why. I tell you honestly if I'm the right fit — and if I'm not, I'll point you somewhere better.

02

Then I build, in the open.

A shared Linear board, Loom updates twice a week, and a staging URL from day one. You watch it come together. No black boxes, no "we're working on it" emails.

03

Finally, I launch — and stick around.

I deploy it, document it, and hand over keys. Plus 30 days of free fixes and a friendly Slack channel for whenever something feels off.

01 / 06 • ~3 days
phase 01 ~30 min call · free

Discovery — figuring out if this is real.

A no-deck, no-pitch call. You walk me through the problem and the people you're solving it for. I ask uncomfortable questions about scope, timeline, and budget early — because finding out later is expensive.

  • →What you'll get: a written recap with my honest read, risks I see, and whether I'm a fit.
  • →What I need: 30 minutes, any context you've got, and an honest budget range.
  • →If we're not a fit: I'll point you to two devs who probably are. Free of charge.
phase 02 ~3 days · fixed-price proposal

Scope — pricing reality, not optimism.

I write a one-page proposal: what we're building, what we're explicitly not building, the timeline with milestones, and a fixed price. No "TBD" line items. No vague AI sprinkled on top.

  • →Deliverable: proposal PDF, signed SOW, 30% deposit invoice.
  • →Milestones: 3–4 named checkpoints with go/no-go decisions you control.
  • →Change requests: handled in writing, priced before any work starts.
phase 03 ~1 week · Figma + flow review

Design — flows before pixels.

If you don't have a designer, I sketch the critical user flows in Figma — login, core action, edge cases. We argue about the boring stuff (empty states, error copy) early so the build phase is just building.

  • →Output: clickable Figma prototype + data model sketch.
  • →Got a designer already? Even better. I'll do a build-feasibility pass and flag anything risky.
  • →Sign-off: short async Loom from you saying "yes, build this."
phase 04 ~3–6 weeks · in the open

Build — staging URL from day one.

You get a Linear board, a Slack channel, and a live staging environment by end of week one. Two Loom updates per week. You see PRs as they merge. No surprises, no theater.

  • →Cadence: Mon plan, Thu demo, Fri staging deploy. Async-first.
  • →Stack defaults: Next.js, TypeScript, Postgres, Vercel — boring tech that scales.
  • →Tests: written alongside code, not "later." CI green or it doesn't ship.
phase 05 ~3–5 days · pre-launch hardening

QA — breaking it before users do.

A hardening sprint: end-to-end tests on critical flows, accessibility audit, perf budget check, real-data load test, and a written security pass. Then we triage every bug into "ship-blocker" or "next sprint."

  • →Checklist: Lighthouse ≥ 90, WCAG AA, error tracking wired, backups tested.
  • →UAT: you and 1–2 real users break the staging app. I fix and re-deploy daily.
  • →Rollback plan: documented, tested, one command away.
phase 06 launch day + 30 days support

Deploy — calm launch, no fireworks.

A boring launch is a good launch. Production deploy with feature flags, monitoring dashboards live, and a runbook for the first 72 hours. Then I hand over keys, ownership, and docs you can actually read.

  • →Handover: repo access, env vars, deployment guide, architecture diagram.
  • →Free for 30 days: any bug introduced by me, I fix on my time.
  • →After that: retainer for new features, or an introduction to a long-term hire.

/ questions?

The stuff people ask first.

How much does a project cost?

Most projects land between $8k and $40k depending on scope. I quote per-project (not per-hour) so you know the number upfront. Smaller things — landing pages, internal tools — start around $3k.

How long does it take?

Real MVPs usually take 4–8 weeks. Bigger builds, 2–4 months. I work with one or two clients at a time so your project actually gets attention, not a slot in a queue.

Do you work alone or with a team?

Solo, by default. For bigger projects I bring in a trusted designer or mobile dev I've shipped with before — you'll meet them up front, no anonymous handoffs.

What if I just have a rough idea?

Perfect — that's most of my clients. We'll spend the first week shaping it into something concrete: scope, priorities, what to cut. You don't need to come with a spec.

What happens after launch?

30 days of free fixes are included. After that, you can keep me on a small monthly retainer, hire me ad-hoc, or take everything in-house — the code is yours, well-documented, no lock-in.
